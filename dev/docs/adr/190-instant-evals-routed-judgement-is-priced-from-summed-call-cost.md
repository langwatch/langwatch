# ADR-190: A routed Instant Evals judgement is priced from the summed cost of its calls, at the one published markup

**Date:** 2026-10-29

**Status:** Accepted

**Amended:** 2026-11-17: customer-facing text names the product, never a backend. A routed result's details read "LangWatch Judge" and the escalation count (decision 10), and unrouted spend rows name the model `langwatch-instant-preview` (decision 6). Rows written before carry `langwatch-evals-preview` (to 2026-11-23) or `jev`. Route table backend ids stay on the judgement's routing, not in customer-facing text.

**Amended:** 2026-11-25: a route entry may carry a difficulty gate (decision 11), an OpenAI Responses backend may set a reasoning effort (decision 12), the judged query hold leaves out backends only scenario judging can reach (decision 7), the scenario judge is a second caller that routes (decision 10), recorded under its own spend model (decision 3), and an LLM backend may be Gemini (`gemini_generate_content`, module ADR-002 decision 9), which reports its tokens in `usageMetadata` (decision 2).

**Amended:** 2026-11-25: judged queries may send the `search` route behind their own flag, under a per-query cost ceiling (decision 13), which resolves "Before queries route".

> One line: when a judge call carries a **route**, the Instant Evals judge may ask a decision model, Jev and an LLM judge, add up what every call cost at deploy-time prices, and charge that sum at the one published markup. Unrouted calls keep the ADR-174 rule.

## Context

- ADR-174 makes an Instant Evals judgement one classifier call, priced from the tokens the classifier billed, with one pricing rule that moved into the judge leaf unchanged (decisions 6, 8 and 13).
- The judge router (PR #9118) adds a second way to answer. A call that carries a route can go to a decision model, to Jev, and to an LLM judge from Anthropic or OpenAI, as an escalation or as a pair. Each leg bills its own input and output tokens, so the classifier's input-only rule no longer describes the cost.
- ADR-174 does not allow this in three places. Decision 6 rejected a second model call and a second billing path. Decision 8 prices the tokens the classifier billed with the existing rule. Decision 13 says the leaf owns one pricing rule and calls only the classifier. Decision 11 reuses `release_instant_evals` over a separate judge flag.
- Nothing in the repository recorded the change before this ADR. The pricing rationale lived only in the flag description, `.env.example`, the router feature and the pull request body.
- Hard rule from ADR-174 holds: customers never supply a classifier key. Every router leg uses LangWatch's own keys, on LangWatch cloud only.

## Decision

1. **A routed call may make several calls.** When a judge call carries a route, the judge leaf may call a decision model, Jev and an LLM judge (escalation or pair). The leaf reaches them through two outbound channels (hosted decisions, and Anthropic, OpenAI or Gemini), with LangWatch's keys, on cloud only.
2. **Each leg is priced from its own tokens.** A leg costs its reported input and output tokens at a deploy-time per-backend price (`INSTANT_EVAL_JUDGE_PRICING`, versioned, with an optional long-context tier). Every backend reports both counts in its response (`usage`, or Gemini's `usageMetadata`), and the leaf carries both from every leg: Jev (`usage.input_tokens`, `usage.output_tokens`), OpenAI's Decisions API (the same two fields, with `output_tokens` documented as 0), and the Anthropic and OpenAI LLM judges. Gemini (2026-11-25) reports them in `usageMetadata` instead: `promptTokenCount` is the input, and `candidatesTokenCount` plus `thoughtsTokenCount` is the output, billed at the output rate. A leg that reports output tokens alone is billed for them. Jev publishes no output price and Decisions charges none, so the default output rate for both is 0; a pricing file that sets one is billed, never ignored. A backend the route can reach that has no price refuses boot. When the variable is unset, the default route prices Jev at its published rate. The hosted classifier on an unrouted call is still priced from its input tokens alone (decision 6).
3. **The customer price is the summed cost times the one markup.** The sum covers every paid leg. The markup is the single published one (1.3). It is recorded as one spend row per request, under the model `langwatch-judge` and the rate version `instant_eval_judge_router@<version>x<markup>` (a scenario judgement: one row per paid verdict and turn decision under `langwatch-judge-preview`, module ADR-002 decisions 4 and 6). Legs paid before a stop (a deadline, a cancel or a failed later leg) are still billed. A judge call records a stop's spend under a freshly minted request id, never the retry key, so the redelivery that completes under the key keeps its own row and every paid attempt is billed. A routed page that reports after its run finished (the cancel grace or the stall wake ended the run while the page was still judging) was paid for but is not in the finish's total, so it is billed on a spend row of its own (`instanteval_<run>_late_page_<n>`), only once the run's finish has landed, and a `late_page_billed` event adds its cost and price to the finished run's row. The row's cost and price therefore always equal what the run's spend rows bill. If the finish has not landed within the hold (15 minutes; it lands in seconds unless its record failed), the held pages are billed without it under the same spend ids and logged at error with their amounts, so a lost finish cannot strand them; a finish that lands later finds nothing held. A late page with no routed spend is left as before the router: neither billed nor added to the row.
4. **A routed judgement is identified by carrying a cost, including 0, and names the tokens that cost covers.** A judgement with a cost field is a routed one; a judgement without one is unrouted. Next to the cost (`costNanoUsd`) travels `routedInputTokens`, the input tokens that cost already covers. It is carried on the `page_judged` event, the run's process state and the finish intent, and reads as 0 on older events. A judgement, page or run costs `costNanoUsd + classifier rate x (inputTokens - routedInputTokens)`, rounded to nano-USD, and its price is that cost at the markup (`instantEvalSpentCostUsd`). Routed tokens are never priced twice, and unrouted tokens are never priced at zero.
5. **A run may mix routed and unrouted calls.** Because each token is billed by the rule that covers it, a run that routes some calls and not others is billed correctly. Resolving the route once per run is still recommended, so one run's spend row names one model and rate version. A routed call whose question kind the table does not route still asks the hosted classifier, and carries that classifier's cost at its routed price.
6. **Unrouted calls keep ADR-174 pricing.** Unrouted calls, runs and judged queries keep the classifier's input-token rule, the `langwatch-instant-preview` model (older rows carry `langwatch-evals-preview` or `jev`) and the `instant_eval@<rate>x<markup>` rate version.
7. **An unrouted judged query is held at the dearest base input rate a search or LLM-judge route can reach.** Before a query is judged, its hold is the query token budget priced at the dearest base input rate over every backend a search or LLM-judge route can reach (enabled entries, their gates and enabled fallbacks), never below the classifier rate, at the one markup. A backend only a scenario judging route reaches is left out (2026-11-25), so a dear scenario judge does not raise every query's hold; it still needs a price to boot. The leaf computes the rate once at boot and exposes it as `InstantEvalJudgeApi.queryHoldPricing()`. With the public jev-only table the hold is unchanged. This rate does not bound a routed query, which may send the same text on several legs (a pair, a fallback, an escalation, a gate send per question), sends the full text once per question to an LLM leg, and pays output tokens and long-context input rates. A routed query is therefore held at its cost ceiling instead (decision 13), and this rate holds unrouted queries only.
8. **Routing is gated per organization by `release_instant_evals_judge_router`,** on by default on LangWatch Cloud and turned off per organization by a targeting rule, so decision 11 of ADR-174 does not cover routing. A self-hosted install (`IS_SAAS` unset) defaults it off through the flag's `selfHostedDefaultValue`: its judgements go through Connect, which carries no route, so it never offers routing unless an operator switches the flag on. The flag is read for the project within its organization, so an organization rule applies.
9. **Rollout.** Routing stays off until a deploy has fully rolled out. The routed event fields (`costNanoUsd`, `routedInputTokens`) are new, and an old pod that reads a new event strips them, which would price a routed judgement from tokens alone. The `late_page_billed` event and the run process's late-page fields (`isFinishRecorded`, `unbilledLatePages`, `lateHoldUntilMs`) are new as well; an old pod neither folds the event nor keeps the fields, and only a routed run produces either.
10. **Two callers route: an LLM-as-judge evaluation on LangWatch Evals (preview), and the scenario judge on LangWatch Judge (preview); judged queries route behind their own flag.** With the flag on, an LLM judge (boolean, score or category) whose model is `langwatch/evals-preview` (or the earlier `langwatch/instant-evals`) and whose project runs on LangWatch's model sends the route `{ job: "llm_judge" }`; the table picks the slot and mode. A judge on `langwatch/instant-preview` is never routed (2026-11-23). A hosted id on a judge states no preference: where the project's Instant Evals column holds its own decision model, that model answers, unrouted. Whether an explicit hosted pick should win over the column is an open product question (2026-11-23). An own decision model is never routed, and the flag is not read for it. The second caller (2026-11-25) is the scenario judge on `langwatch/judge-preview`, gated by `release_langwatch_judge_preview`: each criterion of a verdict sends the route `{ job: "scenario_judge" }` (module ADR-002). Judged queries send the `search` route only behind their own flag (decision 13); Instant Eval runs send no route. A routed result's details name the LangWatch Judge and how many questions escalated, never a backend id, and with the flag on the LLM judge picker offers LangWatch Evals (preview).
11. **A route entry may carry a difficulty gate (2026-11-25).** Sureness tells how sure a backend is of its own answer. A gate asks a separate question first, before any answer: a hosted classifier backend scores how hard the case is to judge, on the levels of a question the code names and freezes (`difficulty_v1`: five levels, read back as the probability-weighted mean level index, 0 the easiest). A score under the entry's `easyBelow` sends the question to the entry's `primary` alone; any other outcome, including a gate that failed, timed out or gave no score, sends it to the entry's `escalateTo` alone. No sureness is read after a gate, so a gated entry takes no `threshold` and no `pairWith`.
    - The gate reads the job, the question's first 3,000 characters and the full text (a text over 12,000 characters as its two ends). The wording and the cut are part of the question's name: a threshold was measured with them, so a change is a new name and a re-measured threshold, never an edit in place.
    - The gate is asked once per question, with no fallback, under its backend's own timeout. While the hosted classifier is down the gate waits out that timeout (the channel's retries and breaker hold) before the question goes the hard way, so a gate backend sets a short `timeoutMs`; skipping the gate at once on an open breaker is not built. It is a paid leg like any other: its tokens are in the summed cost (decision 3), and its backend needs a price and a key to boot (decision 2).
    - A branch whose backend cannot answer is answered by the other backend, flagged (`primary_failed` for the easy branch, `gate_hard_failed` for the hard one). A provider that is down then costs accuracy on those questions, never the judgement. Routing reports the branch (`gate:easy`, `gate:hard`, `gate_no_score`). The hard branch is the gate's routing choice, not an escalation: `escalated` counts only a question the easy branch's failed primary left to the escalation, so a result's details (decision 10) do not read "escalated" on a case the gate sent to the stronger judge on purpose.
    - An entry with any backend switched off is unusable, the gate's included, so the mode falls back to the next cheaper entry. Switching the gate's backend off is the kill switch for a gated entry.
    - Rollout: a release that does not know the field drops it when it reads the table and then refuses to boot ("escalateTo needs a threshold"). A gated table is set only once every pod runs a release with this decision, and unset before a rollback.
12. **An OpenAI Responses backend may set a reasoning effort (2026-11-25).** `reasoningEffort` (`none`, `minimal`, `low`, `medium`, `high`) on a backend is sent as the request's reasoning effort on every call of that backend. Unset, the request names none and is the request sent before the field existed. The table refuses the field on any other backend kind. The provider reports reasoning inside the output tokens, which the leg already bills at the backend's output rate (decision 2). Reasoning counts against the output cap, so any effort but `none` raises it from 8,000 to 32,000 tokens (OpenAI advises reserving 25,000 for reasoning); an answer the provider still stops before the evaluation call is no answer, its tokens billed, and the log names the stop reason. Writing that cap takes minutes, so such a backend's `timeoutMs` defaults to 300,000 (about 110 tokens a second) and the table refuses one below it. An LLM backend may set its own cap, `maxOutputTokens` (reasoning included), sent on every call in place of the default and refused on a Jev or Decisions backend. A reasoning backend that sets one needs the time to write that cap at 50 tokens a second (`timeoutMs >= maxOutputTokens / 50` seconds), which is also its default timeout. 50 is the slow tenth of the stored high-effort search calls measured end to end, prompt read included (72 at the median), on inputs up to about 21,000 tokens; the 300,000 default above assumed 110 and is unchanged. An answer cut at the cap is no answer with its tokens billed, and the backend's fallback answers. The rule makes a cut more likely than a timeout, not certain: a slower call, or a longer input up to the 128,000-token content budget, can still time out first. A backend without its own cap keeps the 300,000 floor (added 2026-11-25 for the search route, decision 13). A call cut off by its timeout or a caller's stop gets no response, so it reads no usage: what the provider charged for it is LangWatch's cost and is never billed (`store: false` leaves no response to fetch later). The floor keeps that rare. Under a query's cost ceiling (decision 13) such a call, or one whose socket was lost, still counts: its send settles at its whole hold. Known limit: the table does not check the value against the model. A model without reasoning refuses the field, GPT-5 refuses `none` and GPT-5.1 and later refuse `minimal`, and o-series models take only `low` to `high`; a refused value fails every call of that backend into its fallbacks, logged with status 400, so set an effort only after one test call on that model. Rollout: a release that does not know the field drops it when it reads the table, boots, and sends no effort, so during a rolling deploy the same table answers at two efforts. Set `reasoningEffort` only once every pod runs a release with this decision, and unset it before a rollback.

13. **A judged query may route, behind its own flag and under a cost ceiling (2026-11-25).** `release_instant_evals_search_router` (default off on LangWatch Cloud and self-hosted, read for the project within its organization) makes a judged LangWatchQL query send the route `{ job: "search" }` on every classify request; the table picks the slot and mode. It is separate from `release_instant_evals_judge_router`, so turning this on routes only queries. Instant Eval runs are not routed: a run's estimate and hold are still priced at the classifier rate. A project on its own decision model drops the route (ADR-182), and a self-hosted install judging through Connect carries none. Accepted loss: on the search route, a reasoning leg that times out falls to the fallback LLM, which is billed, while the timed-out leg's tokens are not. With the leg below, no escalated check in the 2026-11-25 replay timed out (the one answer over the cap is cut and billed); a production call that still times out is LangWatch's cost, at most the leg's worst case.
    - **The ceiling.** A routed query's cost ceiling is its query token budget at **0.15 USD per million tokens** in our cost, a code constant (`INSTANT_EVAL_ROUTED_QUERY_CEILING_USD_PER_MILLION` in `instant-eval-pricing.ts`), not a deploy-time variable: changing it ships in a release. With the default budget of 4,000,000 tokens that is **0.60 USD**, and the query is held at the ceiling times the markup, **0.78 USD**, instead of decision 7's rate. This decision is the one home of these numbers. The unrouted hold at the default budget and the public rate stays 0.2184 USD.
    - **How it binds.** The query hands one ceiling to every request. Before each send (a leg, a fallback, an escalation, a gate send, or the classifier call for a kind the table does not route), the router holds that send's worst case on it, and once the send settles the hold becomes what it was billed, or stays whole for an LLM call that went out with no usage back (a timeout or a lost socket), which the provider charged but nobody can bill. A send that fits only once sends in flight settle waits for them, in the order asked (a caller's stop ends the wait), so a query judging many rows at once is not refused for holds that are about to free. A send is not sent when billed plus its worst case would pass the ceiling even with nothing held: that leg fails with the flag `cost_ceiling:<backend>`, so its fallback (if it fits) is asked, an escalation that does not fit keeps the primary answer (`escalation_failed`), and a text nothing could be sent for is skipped as `query_cost_ceiling`, never as a provider rate limit (an unrouted kind's refusal is logged, since it carries no routing flags). Spend, the unbilled calls included, is therefore at most the ceiling, and the charge at most the hold.
    - **A send's worst case.** One token per UTF-8 byte of what is sent (a token is at least one byte) plus 1,024 tokens of slack per send, at the dearer of the base and long-context input rates; plus, for an LLM, its output cap (its own `maxOutputTokens` where set, else 8,000 tokens, 32,000 with a reasoning effort) at the dearer output rate, and for a decision model 256 output tokens per question. An LLM and Decisions are counted once per question, Jev once for all. The text is measured as its channel fits it (an LLM's 128,000-token content budget, or the budget the questions leave a decision model), so a long trace is held at what can be sent, not at its raw size. A backend with no price is never sent. A send billed past its worst case is logged at error.
    - **Spend** is the routed path of decisions 3 and 4: one spend row per query, model `langwatch-judge`, cost summed over every leg, price at the one markup. A two-leg example: a 2,009-token row with one question that Decisions answers unsure and the LLM leg escalates (2,263 input and 176 output tokens) costs 2,009 x 0.10 + 2,263 x 0.10 + 176 x 0.50 per million = 0.000515 USD, priced 0.000670 USD. The text is billed once per leg that reads it, never by a rate times summed legs. The shipped route averages 0.110 USD per million row tokens in cost (0.143 at the markup) on the benchmark size mix, varying per row.
    - **The shipped search route** (deploy-time `INSTANT_EVAL_JUDGE_ROUTES` and `INSTANT_EVAL_JUDGE_PRICING`; model ids and keys are deploy secrets, not shown):

      | Backend        | Kind                 | Input / output USD per 1M | Timeout                              | Output cap                       | Fallbacks      | Reasoning effort |
      | -------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------- | -------------- | ---------------- |
      | `decisions`    | `decisions`          | 0.10 / 0                  | 30 s                                 | n/a                              | none           | n/a              |
      | `llm_high`     | `openai_responses`   | 0.10 / 0.50               | 22 s (the cap at 50 tokens a second) | 1,100 tokens (`maxOutputTokens`) | `llm_fallback` | `high`           |
      | `llm_fallback` | `anthropic_messages` | 0.10 / 0.50               | 30 s                                 | 8,000 tokens (default)           | none           | n/a              |

      Every `routes.search` slot (each bucket and `arithmetic`) is `{ "instant": { "primary": "decisions", "escalateTo": "llm_high", "threshold": 0.40 } }` and names no other mode, so every mode falls back to it. Decisions answers every yes-or-no question; one with sureness `|2p - 1|` under 0.40 (about the least-sure 10%) goes to the OpenAI Responses leg at high effort, and its failure, an answer cut at its 1,100-token cap, or a call past its 22 s timeout goes to the Anthropic leg. Score and category questions stay on the hosted classifier unless `kinds` routes them (decision 5). The other jobs' slots are unchanged. Prices are list prices as of 2026-11-25, to be confirmed before deploy.

    - **The 22 s leg (2026-11-25).** The route decision asked for the Anthropic fallback after 10 s on the high-effort leg, but 10 s at the measured 50 tokens a second writes only 500 tokens. In the stored high-effort answers on the escalated search checks (88 checks, reasoning included) the output was 199 tokens at p50, 508 at p90 and 1,141 at most, so a 500-token cap would cut about 1 in 10 of them. The leg therefore keeps the 1,100-token cap and waits 22 s. Replayed through the router, no verdict changes (78.7 balanced accuracy, as without the cap), the one answer over 1,100 tokens is cut and billed instead of timing out, and no escalated call times out. The p95 wait over all checks stays 2.9 s; over the escalated checks it is 9.7 s against 9.0 s with the 10 s timeout. 500 tokens and 10 s also keeps accuracy in the replay (78.7) but swaps about 1 in 10 high-effort answers for the fallback's, a route that was not the one analysed.
    - **Rollout.** The ceiling travels in process with the request and is on no event, so an old pod during a rolling deploy simply sends no route (it does not know the flag). Turn the flag on only after the release has fully rolled out, and only once the search slots above are in the deployed table; with the public jev-only table a routed query is answered and billed as today, at the routed model name.

## Relation to ADR-174

This record supersedes ADR-174 in part:

- decision 6, for routed calls (a second call and a second cost basis are allowed there);
- the pricing bullet of decision 8, for routed calls;
- decision 11, for routing (a separate flag).

It amends decision 13. The leaf now owns two pricing rules and five upstreams: the hosted classifier, the hosted decisions endpoint, Anthropic, OpenAI and Gemini. It still calls no peer Api.

## Resolved questions

Both were answered with the first option.

1. **Does the 1.3 markup apply to third-party LLM cost?** Yes: one published markup on the summed cost. The markup was set for Jev at its published rate. The router applies it to the summed cost, so the cost of an Anthropic or OpenAI leg is marked up at 1.3 too. The alternative is a separate markup per backend, which needs a field on the price table (it has none today).
2. **Does the router ship dark, or with one caller behind the flag?** One caller behind the flag (decision 10). Before this decision no non-test code set a route, so the router, its flag and its event fields were unreachable in production. The caller that now ships is `EvaluationJudgingService`: it sends the `llm_judge` route when `release_instant_evals_judge_router` is on. One option is to ship dark and add the caller in a later pull request. The other is to wire one caller, read the flag where the judged rows build their classify input, and ship it behind `release_instant_evals_judge_router`.

## Before queries route

Resolved by decision 13 (2026-11-25), with the second option. Decision 7's hold is not a cap, so before a judged query routed one of these had to land, with tests:

- Price the hold at the worst case the route can reach: the dearest rate including the long-context tier, times the route's maximum re-sends, plus an output-token allowance per LLM call.
- Or pass a cost ceiling into the router, so a routed call stops sending once its summed cost reaches the hold.

The first was rejected: the output allowance of a reasoning LLM (32,000 tokens per question) alone puts the worst case for a full query far above the one dollar free budget, so every free organization's routed query would be refused.

## Rationale / Trade-offs

One summed price keeps the customer's bill to a single number per judgement, and the ledger to a single row per request. Pricing each leg at the cost we pay, and applying one markup to the sum, avoids a price table the customer would need to read. The cost is that a cheap path and an expensive path share one markup, which is open question 1.

Carrying a cost, including 0, as the marker of a routed judgement avoids a separate flag on every event. A zero-cost routed judgement (every leg cost nothing) is still routed and is not priced from tokens. Carrying `routedInputTokens` next to the cost lets a page or run that mixes routed and unrouted judgements bill each token once, by the rule that covers it, instead of requiring a run to route all calls or none. The price is one more field to sum wherever a run or page is priced.

Billing legs paid before a stop is the same rule ADR-174 applies to a call cancelled after the classifier answered: the upstream was paid, so the customer is charged.

Rejected: a second judge module for the router. Evaluation and Instant Evals would then depend on two leaves, and the pricing rule would still be split.

## Consequences

- The judge leaf has two pricing rules, chosen by whether a judgement carries a cost. Spend rows have a second model name and rate version, which gateway and the ledger already accept as free text.
- Deploy-time JSON now sets prices (`INSTANT_EVAL_JUDGE_PRICING`) and routes (`INSTANT_EVAL_JUDGE_ROUTES`). A price or route change needs a deploy, and the rate version in the ledger tells a replay from a re-rating.
- A run's cost and price are the sum of routed cost and unrouted token price, both marked up once.
- The page budget check reads a running row that stores no routed token count, so it prices every token at the classifier rate on top of the routed cost. That is an upper bound on the bill, never an under-count.
- Once a deploy sets a route table whose search or LLM-judge routes (or a routed kind's default) reach an LLM backend, judged query holds rise to that LLM's rate, even before a caller routes queries. A backend only scenario judging reaches does not raise them (decision 7). A free organization's query can be refused earlier. That is the conservative side.
- An organization without the flag is priced the ADR-174 way. With it, its LLM-judge evaluations on LangWatch Evals (preview) are priced by decision 3.
- With `release_instant_evals_search_router` on, a free organization needs 0.78 USD of room for a judged query at the default budget instead of 0.2184 USD, and a query near its ceiling answers some texts from their first answer or skips them as `query_cost_ceiling` instead of spending more (decision 13).
