# Domains: fewer, coarser modules in one fixed order

Date: 2026-10-06. Status: WITHDRAWN (Alex, 2026-10-06 evening). Alex asked only about the commercial modules; entitlement and
usage merge into licensing (rulings file). The rest of this document is kept for its numbers only.

## 1. The principle (Alex, 2026-10-06)

Core may call enterprise. The core/enterprise split exists only to gate licensing: self-hosted
enterprise features and the Cloud SaaS features. It is not a layer and says nothing about who may call
whom.

## 2. What is wrong today

- 65 modules; **40 of them sit in one cycle**, with 248 cycle findings (246 after today's role and
  feature-flag cut).
- The edge-by-edge plan needs 52 cuts, about 12 new fact types and 5 read models with backfills, and
  every cut forces a decision (R1 to R10 were ten of them). Many cuts exist only because one concept is
  split across several modules that call each other: plan, usage, billing and licensing; auth, identity,
  user and sso; organization, project and role; evaluation, evaluator, monitor and experiment.
- Merging alone is not the fix: merging the four commerce modules removes 11 findings. **Direction is the
  fix**: once those four call nothing above them, 35 findings go, merged or not.

## 3. The proposal: eight domains, one order

A domain is a group of modules that serve one concept. Domains sit in a fixed order; a module may call
modules in its own domain or any domain below it, never above. The order below is the one that needs the
fewest cuts across the current edges (searched over every ordering).

| # (bottom first) | Domain        | Modules today                                                                             | Concept                                                             |
| ---------------- | ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1                | tenancy       | organization, project, role, feature-flag, data-retention, data-privacy, audit-log, share | who the customer is, their projects, settings, policies and history |
| 2                | commerce      | entitlement, usage, billing, licensing                                                    | what they bought, what it allows, what they used                    |
| 3                | identity      | auth, identity, user, sso, scim, api-key                                                  | who is calling and how they proved it                               |
| 4                | providers     | model-provider                                                                            | model credentials and routing inputs                                |
| 5                | evaluation    | evaluation, evaluator, monitor, instant-eval, experiment, dataset, workflow               | judging outputs and running experiments                             |
| 6                | observability | trace, log, metric, analytics, topic, annotation, coding-agent, github                    | what happened in the customer's agents                              |
| 7                | agents        | agent, scenario, suite, prompt                                                            | building and testing agents                                         |
| 8                | gateway       | gateway, enterprise-gateway, governance                                                   | the AI gateway and its governance                                   |

The 25 modules outside today's cycle (authz, notification, secret, stored-object, slack, webhook,
presence, managed-provider, ops, langy, onboarding, dashboard, automation and the rest) keep their place:
foundations below tenancy, surfaces above gateway.

### 3.2 Commerce, concretely (the R4 question)

One module, `commerce`, owns the whole question "what did this customer buy, what does it allow, how much
have they used":

- **Plan:** resolves the plan from `packages/plans` (catalogue and overrides) and the purchase: a Stripe
  subscription on Cloud, a signed licence on self-hosted, a bespoke contract for connected customers.
- **Limits and enforcement:** today's `EntitlementApi` (getActivePlan, getUsage, assertWithinUsageLimit,
  requestBound, resolvePlanNextStep, listOrganizationSpend). Its 19 callers keep the same operations under
  the new token.
- **Metering:** `billable_events` and the trace meter (today's usage), threshold facts and the warning
  emails (sent through notification).
- **Billing:** subscriptions, invoices, seats, connected self-hosted invoicing (today's billing).
- **Licences and Connect:** validation, storage, issuing, activation codes, Connect services, contract
  budgets, usage reports and the self-hosted instance registry (today's licensing, 57 operations).

It is classified enterprise (the split is only about gating). Without a licence or a Cloud subscription it
answers the self-hosted baseline plan, so open-source installs keep working. It calls only tenancy and
foundations (authz, notification); everything that needs organisation or project details below it reads
them from tenancy. R4, D1 (usage facts), E1 and E2 dissolve: there is no cross-module plan read left to
cut, and no snapshot fact, read model or backfill is needed.

If one module is too large, the fallback is two: `plan` (limits, metering, enforcement) above `billing`
(subscriptions, licences, Connect), with plan calling billing synchronously and billing calling nothing
above it.
