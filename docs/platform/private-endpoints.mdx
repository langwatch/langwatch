<Note>
  Private endpoints are for LangWatch Cloud. A self-hosted LangWatch runs in
  your own network and reaches a private model gateway directly, so this page
  does not apply to it.
</Note>

## What it is

Some teams run their own model gateway, such as a LiteLLM proxy or another
OpenAI-compatible service, inside a private network with no public endpoint. A
private endpoint lets LangWatch Cloud call that gateway over AWS PrivateLink.
The traffic stays on the AWS network and never crosses the public internet.

The work is split in two:

- **LangWatch staff** create the PrivateLink connection to your gateway and
  register it as a private endpoint for your organization.
- **You** choose the hostname LangWatch uses for it, in **Settings > Private
  endpoints**, and add a custom model provider that points at that hostname.

You can change hostnames yourself at any time. You need LangWatch again only
when the PrivateLink connection itself changes.

## When to use it

Use a private endpoint when all of these are true:

- You use LangWatch Cloud.
- Your model gateway runs in AWS and has no public endpoint.
- The gateway speaks the OpenAI API, for example LiteLLM or vLLM.

If your gateway already has a public HTTPS endpoint, add it as a [custom model
provider](/platform/model-providers) instead. You do not need a private
endpoint.

## How it works

1. Your gateway sits behind an internal Application Load Balancer (ALB) that
   holds a TLS certificate for a hostname such as `llm.internal.example.com`.
2. A Network Load Balancer (NLB) in front of that ALB is shared as a VPC
   endpoint service.
3. LangWatch connects an interface endpoint in its own AWS account to your
   endpoint service, and registers it for your organization.
4. You bind `llm.internal.example.com` to that private endpoint in LangWatch.
5. A model provider with the base URL `https://llm.internal.example.com/v1`
   now reaches your gateway over PrivateLink. LangWatch uses the hostname for
   TLS, so your certificate is checked as usual.

## Before you start

- Ask your LangWatch contact for two values: the AWS account to allow, and
  the AWS region LangWatch connects from. This page does not list them.
- Your gateway must serve HTTPS on port 443 behind an internal ALB.
- The ALB's certificate must be issued by a public certificate authority, for
  example a public certificate from AWS Certificate Manager. The hostname does
  not need a public DNS record. LangWatch checks the certificate against the
  public trust store and cannot trust a private certificate authority.
- To bind a hostname in LangWatch, you need the `organization:manage`
  permission. Organization administrators have it.

## 1. Set up the endpoint service in AWS

Do these steps in the AWS account and region where your gateway runs.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Put a Network Load Balancer in front of the ALB">
    Create an internal NLB in the same VPC as the ALB. Create a target group
    of the **Application Load Balancer** type, on protocol TCP and port 443,
    and register your internal ALB as its target. Add a TCP listener on port
    443 that forwards to this target group.

    The NLB passes TLS through untouched. The ALB keeps terminating TLS with
    your certificate.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Allow the traffic through your security groups">
    The ALB's security group must accept TCP 443 from the NLB. If the NLB has
    a security group, it must accept TCP 443 for PrivateLink traffic, or you
    can turn off inbound rule checks for PrivateLink traffic on the NLB.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create a VPC endpoint service on the NLB">
    In the VPC console, open **Endpoint services** and create a service for
    the NLB. Keep **Acceptance required** turned on, so that you approve each
    connection.

    If LangWatch connects from a different region than yours, add that region
    under **Supported Regions**. This turns on cross-region PrivateLink for the
    service.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Allow LangWatch's AWS account">
    On the endpoint service, open **Allow principals** and add the AWS account
    your LangWatch contact gave you, as
    `arn:aws:iam::<account-id>:root`. Only that account can request a
    connection.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send LangWatch two values">
    Send your LangWatch contact:

    - the **service name** of the endpoint service, which starts with
      `com.amazonaws.vpce.`;
    - the **hostname** your certificate covers, for example
      `llm.internal.example.com`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Accept the connection request">
    When LangWatch creates its side of the connection, a request appears under
    **Endpoint connections** on your endpoint service. Accept it. Until you do,
    the connection stays pending and calls cannot reach your gateway.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## 2. What LangWatch does

LangWatch staff create the interface endpoint for your service and register it
as a private endpoint for your organization. A private endpoint has a name
and a region, for example "Model gateway" in `eu-west-1`.

The network addresses of the endpoint and the port it accepts are set by
LangWatch staff only. You never enter an address, and LangWatch never shows
you one.

When this is done, your LangWatch contact tells you, and the endpoint appears
in **Settings > Private endpoints**.

## 3. Bind your hostname

Open **Settings > Private endpoints**. The page lists the private endpoints
LangWatch registered for your organization, with their name, region, status
and bound hostnames. Before LangWatch registers one, the page is empty.

A hostname must be a full name, such as `llm.internal.example.com`. These are
refused:

| You enter | LangWatch says |
|-----------|----------------|
| An IP address, such as `10.0.0.5` | Enter a hostname, not an IP address. |
| A wildcard, such as `*.example.com` | Wildcards are not supported; bind each hostname. |
| A single word, such as `gateway` | Enter a fully qualified hostname, such as llm.example.com. |
| `localhost` | localhost cannot be bound. |
| A hostname you already bound | llm.internal.example.com is already bound in this organization. |

You can bind more than one hostname to the same endpoint, for example one per
certificate name.

## 4. Add the model provider

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Model providers">
    Open **Settings > Model providers** and click **Add model provider**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the custom provider">
    Choose **Custom (OpenAI-compatible)**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter the base URL and key">
    Set **CUSTOM_BASE_URL** to `https://llm.internal.example.com/v1`, with
    your own hostname. Enter the key your gateway expects in
    **CUSTOM_API_KEY**, for example a LiteLLM virtual key. Add the models your
    gateway serves under **Custom models**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save and test">
    Save, then use **Test Connection** in the row menu. The test runs through
    the private endpoint.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Security model

- **Only your organization can use your private endpoint.** A binding belongs
  to one organization. If another organization enters the same hostname, its
  calls do not reach your endpoint. They are resolved through public DNS like
  any other address.
- **LangWatch never looks your hostname up in DNS.** A call to a bound
  hostname goes straight to the private endpoint LangWatch registered for you.
  The hostname is used only for TLS and the `Host` header. Changing a public
  DNS record cannot redirect your calls.
- **Calls use HTTPS on the registered port only.** A call to a bound hostname
  over `http`, or on any other port, is refused before a connection opens.
- **IP addresses are never accepted.** You bind names, not addresses, and a
  base URL that is an IP address is never treated as bound. You cannot point
  a call at anything in LangWatch's network other than your own endpoint.
- **The certificate is always checked.** LangWatch verifies your gateway's
  certificate against the hostname, as for any public provider.
- **Bindings cover model calls only.** Workflow HTTP blocks, webhooks and HTTP
  agent targets in Agent Testing ignore bindings. They cannot reach your
  private endpoint.

## Limits

Private endpoints are new. These parts of LangWatch use a bound hostname
today:

- saving a provider and **Test Connection**;
- listing the models a provider serves;
- the prompt playground and prompts;
- LLM and evaluator blocks in Workflows;
- evaluators from the built-in evaluator library, such as LLM-as-a-judge and
  RAGAS evaluators;
- topic clustering;
- Agent Testing: the simulator, the judge, prompt and workflow targets, and
  agent test runs;
- the LangWatch AI Gateway, through virtual keys.

Only the **Custom (OpenAI-compatible)** provider, and providers that take a
custom base URL such as OpenAI or Anthropic, can use a private endpoint.
Azure OpenAI, Bedrock, Voyage AI and Codex cannot. A call from one of those to
a bound hostname is refused with a message that the credentials cannot use a
private endpoint yet. Realtime (voice) sessions cannot use a private endpoint
either.

Vertex AI cannot use a private endpoint. It has no base URL setting, so its
calls always go to Google's own endpoint.

If you run LangWatch yourself, the evaluators service must be able to reach
the NLP service. The Helm chart sets this up for you.

## Troubleshooting

| What you see | What it means | What to do |
|--------------|---------------|------------|
| `is bound to a private endpoint that accepts port 443 only` | The base URL names another port, such as `:8443`. | Remove the port from the base URL. |
| `is bound to a private endpoint and must be reached over https` | The base URL starts with `http://`. | Use `https://`. |
| `could not connect to the private endpoint` | LangWatch reached its side of the connection, but your gateway did not answer. | Check that you accepted the connection request, that the NLB target group shows the ALB as healthy, and that the security groups allow TCP 443. |
| A TLS or certificate error that names your hostname | The certificate on the ALB does not cover the hostname, or a private certificate authority issued it. | Bind the exact name the certificate covers, or use a public certificate. |
| The call is refused as a private address | The hostname is not bound in this organization, so LangWatch resolved it through DNS and found a private address. | Check the spelling on **Settings > Private endpoints**, and check that the provider belongs to a project in the same organization. |
| The endpoint is missing from **Settings > Private endpoints** | LangWatch has not registered it yet, or you are in another organization. | Ask your LangWatch contact. |

Error messages never include the private endpoint's addresses. Send your
LangWatch contact the hostname, the time of the call and the full error text
if you need help.
