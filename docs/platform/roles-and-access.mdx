LangWatch describes access with two words.

- A **role** is what you may do: a named set of `resource:action` permissions, such as `traces:view` or `datasets:manage`.
- A **grant** is who holds a role, and where: one principal, one role, one scope.

For the permission catalogue and the built-in organization and team roles, read [Access Control (RBAC)](/platform/rbac).

## Roles

Every organization has three built-in roles, addressed by stable ids: `admin`, `member` and `viewer`. They cannot be changed or deleted; trying answers 409 `role_is_built_in`.

A custom role is one your organization defines, when the built-in roles are not the shape you need. It is addressed by its id. Custom roles are an Enterprise feature.

`GET /api/v1/roles` lists the built-in roles first, then the custom ones. `?builtIn=true` lists only the built-in roles, `?builtIn=false` only the custom ones. From the CLI:

```bash
langwatch roles list               # both
langwatch roles list --built-in    # admin, member and viewer
langwatch roles list --no-built-in # custom roles only
```

## Grants

A grant names three things.

| Part      | Options                                              |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Principal | A user, a group, or an API key                       |
| Role      | `admin`, `member`, `viewer`, or a custom role's id   |
| Scope     | The organization, one team, or one project           |

A grant at the organization reaches every team and project in it; a grant on a team reaches that team and its projects; a grant on a project reaches that project only. A grant can carry an end date, after which it no longer gives access.

Grants are managed with the [Grants API](/api-reference/grants/overview) and `langwatch grants`, and in the product under **Settings > Roles**.

## You cannot grant more than you hold

You can only grant a role whose permissions you hold yourself, at that scope. The same holds when you change a grant's role, and when you create a custom role or add permissions to one: you cannot add a permission you do not hold. Renaming a role, or keeping permissions it already has, is not held against you.

The rule applies to every principal, yourself included, so a grant cannot be used to raise your own access. It is enforced in one place and every door reaches it: the Grants API, the Role Bindings API and the product.

The caller is checked as itself:

- a person is judged by what they hold, directly and through their groups;
- a request made with an **API key** is judged by that key's own permissions, never by those of the person who owns it. A key narrower than its owner can grant only what the key holds.

An expired grant does not count towards what you hold.

A refusal is 403 `grant_exceeds_caller_permissions` for a grant, or `role_exceeds_caller_permissions` for a role, and `meta.missingPermissions` lists what you lack. The CLI prints the list under **Details**.

## Role bindings are deprecated

Grants replace role bindings. `/api/v1/role-bindings` keeps working and reads and writes the same records, but every answer carries a `Deprecation: true` header and a `Link` header naming `/api/grants` as its successor, and `langwatch role-bindings` warns on stderr. See [the migration note](/api-reference/role-bindings/overview#migrating-to-grants).
