## How access is structured

A permission is a `resource:action` pair, for example `traces:view` or `datasets:manage`. A role is a named set of permissions. A role binding gives a role to a person, a group or an API key at one scope: the organization, a team or a project.

LangWatch has three scopes:

| Scope | What it contains |
|-------|------------------|
| **Organization** | Billing, members, seats, groups, roles, the audit log and the organization-wide settings |
| **Team** | A set of projects and the people who work on them |
| **Project** | Traces, evaluations, prompts, datasets, API keys and everything else you send data to |

Every member of the organization has an organization role. Access to a team or a project comes from a team role, given per team or per project through a role binding.

## Organization roles

Set the organization role per member at **Settings > Members**. The role decides what a person can do at the organization level and how their seat is counted.

| Role | What it grants |
|------|----------------|
| **Admin** | Every permission on every team and project in the organization, plus the organization settings: members, teams, roles, groups, billing, the audit log, AI Governance, webhook endpoints and spend data |
| **Member** | `organization:view` and `aiTools:view`. Access to a team or a project comes only from a team role |
| **Lite Member** | `organization:view` and `aiTools:view`. On a team or a project a Lite Member is capped at the Viewer permissions below, plus `annotations:create` and `annotations:update`, and never sees costs, the AI Gateway pages or the audit log |

A Lite Member uses a Lite seat, which is counted separately from full seats. In the settings pages a Lite Member can only be given the Viewer role on a team or a project. A group binding above Viewer applies as Viewer for a Lite Member.

**Also check:** [Members and Teams](/platform/members-and-teams) for seats, invites and how to change a member's role.

## Team roles

A team role applies to one team and, through inheritance, to every project in it. A binding on a single project overrides the team role for that project only. The built-in roles are **Admin**, **Member** and **Viewer**.

## Actions

| Action | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| `view` | Read the resource |
| `create` | Create a new one |
| `update` | Edit an existing one |
| `delete` | Delete one |
| `manage` | Implies `view`, `create`, `update`, `delete`, `rotate`, `attach` and `detach` on the same resource |
| `share` | Create a public share link for a trace |
| `rotate` | Rotate a virtual key |
| `attach`, `detach` | Attach a guardrail to a virtual key, or detach it |
| `viewOtherPersonal` | See the personal virtual keys of other members, for off-boarding |

`share` and `viewOtherPersonal` are never implied by `manage`. Grant them explicitly.

## Custom roles

A custom role is a named permission set you define. Create it at **Settings > Roles & Permissions** with `organization:manage`. Custom roles are Enterprise.

<Frame>
<img src="/images/platform/roles-list.png" alt="Settings > Roles & Permissions with the built-in roles and one custom role" />
</Frame>

A custom role has:

| Field | Rule |
|-------|------|
| Name | Required, 1 to 50 characters, unique in the organization |
| Description | Optional |
| Permissions | One or more `resource:action` pairs |

The permission selector groups the checkboxes by resource. Ticking `manage` ticks the actions it implies. Ticking `create`, `update` or `delete` also ticks `view`.

<Frame>
<img src="/images/platform/roles-create-permissions.png" alt="The Create Role dialog with the permission selector" />
</Frame>

The selector offers these resources: traces, cost, scenarios, annotations, analytics, evaluations, datasets, triggers, workflows, experiments, prompts, secrets, audit log, team, project, governance, ingestion sources, anomaly rules, compliance export, activity monitor and AI tools. The [Roles API](/api-reference/roles/overview) accepts every resource and action in the tables above.

To give a custom role to a person, pick it in the role select at **Settings > Teams & Projects** (a team member row, **Add to team** or **Add access to project**), in the member dialog at **Settings > Members**, in the team assignments of an invite, or bind it to a group. A custom role can be bound at organization, team or project scope. An organization Admin who also holds a custom team role keeps the Admin access.

## Role bindings

A role binding is one row: who, which role, at which scope.

| Part | Options |
|------|---------|
| Who | A user, a group, or an API key |
| Role | Admin, Member, Viewer, or a custom role |
| Scope | The organization, one team, or one project |

You create bindings for users from **Settings > Members** and **Settings > Teams & Projects**, and for groups from **Settings > Groups**. **Settings > Role Bindings** lists every binding in the organization per user and per group, filtered by organization, team or project scope. The page is read only.

<Frame>
<img src="/images/platform/role-bindings.png" alt="Settings > Role Bindings listing bindings per person and group" />
</Frame>

An API key can hold bindings too. A key with restricted permissions stores its scopes as bindings on the key, and LangWatch intersects them with the permissions of the user who owns the key on every request. A key never grants more than its owner holds at that moment. A service key with no owner has no such ceiling.

**Also check:** [API Keys](/platform/api-keys) for how to scope a key.

## Groups

A group gives the same bindings to many people at once. Create a group at **Settings > Groups** (Enterprise), add members, and bind roles to the group at any scope. A binding on a group applies to every member of the group, and the members page shows it as "via" the group.

A group synced from your identity provider through SCIM keeps its name and membership under the identity provider's control: you bind roles to it in LangWatch, and the identity provider decides who is in it.

## How a request is authorized

LangWatch first checks that the caller is an active member of the organization; a disabled member is refused, and API keys skip this check. It then collects every binding on the scope chain of the request, the project, its team and the organization, including bindings held through groups. Any binding that grants the permission allows the request; a narrower binding never cancels a wider one. An organization-scoped Admin binding grants every permission. At organization scope, every active member holds the Member organization permissions. Organization-only resources need an organization-scoped binding.
