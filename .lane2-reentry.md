## THE DIAGNOSIS (kept — this is why the fix looks the way it does)

**The merge is semantically broken even though it compiles and typechecks.** One
integration test fails, and it is OURS, and it is a real regression — not flake:

```
FAIL groupQueue.decodeDrop.integration.test.ts
  > given a body-present GQ2 drop (#720 blob lifetime) > when the job is dead-lettered
  > extends the blob holder past the dead-letter quarantine window
AssertionError: expected 3599903 to be greater than 604794902
Test Files  1 failed | 10 passed (11)
     Tests  1 failed | 304 passed | 4 todo (309)
```

`3599903 ms` ≈ **1 hour**. `604794902 ms` ≈ **7 days** (the DLQ window).

**Mechanism (verified in the merged tree, not inferred):**
main landed **#6028 "bound blob retention with a release grace window"**
(`a161cda080`). It added `BLOB_RELEASE_GRACE_TTL_SECONDS = 60 * 60`
(`blobConstants.ts:55`) and `gqGraceExpireIfUnleased` (`blobGraceLua.ts:16-30`),
which on the LAST lease release `EXPIRE`s both the blob key and the legacy holder
key down to **1 hour**.

Our #720 path does, in `groupQueue.ts` order:
- `:1815` / `:2052` `preserveForDlq(...)` → pushes the holder out to the 7-day DLQ window
- `:1820` / `:2057` `writeJobToDlq(...)`
- `:2085` `releaseLease({ values: [jobDataJson], groupId })` ← **post-#6028 this now
  fires the grace window and CLAWS THE HOLDER BACK DOWN TO 1 HOUR**

Net effect: **the dead-letter entry (7d) outlives the blob it references (1h)** — a
drain would recover an envelope pointing at nothing. That is precisely the failure
#720 exists to prevent, reintroduced by main's grace window. The two changes are
individually correct and jointly wrong; nobody would see it without this test.

**#6028 has a SECOND clawback, and it rules out the cheap fixes.** Besides the
release-time grace, `blobSweepLua.ts:87-93` runs a maintenance sweep that
independently repairs any blob it finds:

```lua
redis.call("ZREMRANGEBYSCORE", KEYS[1], "-inf", nowMs)   -- prune expired deadlines
if redis.call("ZCARD", KEYS[1]) > 0 then return "leased" end
...
if ttl == -1 or ttl > ${BLOB_RELEASE_GRACE_TTL_SECONDS} then
  redis.call("EXPIRE", KEYS[3], ${BLOB_RELEASE_GRACE_TTL_SECONDS})   -- back to 1h
  redis.call("EXPIRE", KEYS[2], ${BLOB_RELEASE_GRACE_TTL_SECONDS})
  return "repaired"
```

Read the gate carefully — **the sweep keys ONLY off `ZCARD` of the lease set. It
does not consult `legacyHolderKey` at all.** Consequences:

- ❌ **Skip `releaseLease`** — defeated. The sweep prunes by *logical deadline*
  first, so once the job's lease score passes (routine lease window, shorter than
  7 days) the blob reads as unleased and gets repaired to 1h.
- ❌ **Add a legacy-holder member** — defeated. A `legacyKey` member satisfies the
  *release-time* helper (`SCARD legacyKey > 1`) but the sweep never looks at it.
- ❌ **Re-extend after release** — race by construction, and the sweep undoes it anyway.
- ✅ **Hold a real LEASE for the dead-letter, dated to the quarantine window.**
  Only a ZSET member with a future deadline satisfies BOTH gates
  (`gqGraceExpireIfUnleased`'s `ZCARD > 0` AND the sweep's `ZCARD > 0`).

**Planned shape** — a `BlobLeases.holdForDlq({projectId, hash, tier, ttlSeconds})`
that `preserveForDlq` calls instead of `extendTtl`:
1. `ZADD` a stable DLQ-holder member with score `now + ttlSeconds*1000` — this is
   what makes the blob *leased* for the window and immune to both clawbacks;
2. the same three `EXPIRE`s `extendTtl` already does (leaseKey, legacyHolderKey,
   and — redis tier only — the blob key), because `take`/`renew` cap the PHYSICAL
   TTL at the hardcoded `BLOB_LEASE_SET_TTL_SECONDS`/`BLOB_BACKSTOP_TTL_SECONDS`
   no matter what `ttlSeconds` you pass (this is `extendTtl`'s whole reason to
   exist — see its doc-comment; do not "simplify" it back into `renew`).

Everything then expires together at the 7-day window — bounded, no orphan leak,
and the job's own `releaseLease` can still run untouched since it removes only its
own member. Keep `extendTtl` if nothing else uses it; otherwise fold it in.

⚠ Do NOT "fix" this by weakening the test. Its last assertion
(`holderPttl > 6 days`) is the deliberate falsifiability guard; the previous
assertion compares against the ACTUAL DLQ TTL rather than a magic constant.
